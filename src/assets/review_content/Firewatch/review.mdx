## <u>SYNOPSIS</u>

The year is **1989**. You are a man named **Henry** who has retreated from his messy life to work as a fire lookout in the Wyoming wilderness. Perched high in a mountain, it’s your job to look for smoke and keep the wilderness safe. An especially hot, dry summer has everyone on edge. Your supervisor **Delilah** is available to you at all times over a small, handheld radio. She's your only contact with the world you've left behind. But when something strange draws you out of your lookout tower and into the forest, you’ll explore a wild and unknown environment, facing questions and making choices that can build or destroy the only meaningful relationship you have.

Altough being "only" a few hours long, this is perfectly enough for everything this game aspires to do and be.

From being amazed by the beauty of the forest, it's wildlife and the sunsets, enjoying the fun part of socializing with Delilah, but not only. 

You will have to face your own personnal problems, be honest with yourself, all that while having to deal with the weird things happening in that forest.

<img {...screenshots[1]} class="review-screenshot" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

Gameplay wise, the tempo is pretty slow, and for a good reason. The whole point of this game is to TAKE YOUR TIME, look around you, breathe, listen to the sounds the forest has to offer.
I've stopped my playthrough more than a few times to just listen to the amazing soundtrack while contemplating the sunset, sometimes thinking of Henry's life and problems, but sometimes also of mine.

<img {...screenshots[2]} class="review-screenshot" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

The map system is super original, unlike a lot of games, the map isn't a tab in a menu. You will actualy have a physical paper map, and will have to follow directions.

Rich Sommer (Henry) and Cissy Jones (Delilah) delivered a wonderful voice acting performance, that is perfectly aligned with the whole mood of the game. There is not a single voice line that feels exagerated. 

<img {...screenshots[3]} class="review-screenshot" loading="lazy" decoding="async" />

On the technical side, i had no major issues for the entirety of my playthrough. 
The only problem that i encoutered was that a high framerate can lead to some movements behavioring weirdly. 
So i would advise you to cap it max at 144 (240 is still playable, but the movement issue can be frustrating over time)

If you're looking for a short, slow/chill and emotionally rich game, Firewatch is a must have.
